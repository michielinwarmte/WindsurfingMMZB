# trainer (F5 of AI_FLEET_PLAN.md): the candy along the course, the curriculum, PPO over the bridge

Built 21 October 2026 (Session 53) by Claude Fable 5.1 itself, on `f5/trainer` (Max: "I have a lot of Fable left, so you can do more work instead of the agents"). It is the training design of AI_FLEET_PLAN.md D6b, built in full; the nights of training are the next step.

## What was built

**1. The reward follows the course (`Game/race/sailing_distance.gd`, `race_env.gd`).** The sailing distance from a point to a point is the length of the shortest way a board can sail there: straight when the target can be laid, the upwind distance over the cosine of the beat's track angle (50 degrees) inside the tacking cone, the same with the run's track (130 degrees) inside the gybing cone. It is a true distance (the triangle inequality holds; a unit test tries 500 random triangles), so the reward, **the fall of the sailing distance still to go round the course** (to the next step's point, then the legs between the steps' points), pays the same whichever way the board goes between two places, pays nothing for a detour, and can only ever pay (never charge) for completing a step. A mark's point is its **rounding point**, 20 m past the mark along its rounding ray on the correct side, not the mark itself, so the rounding is paid for; a line is crossed at its best point for the way on (a ternary search along the line). Inside the tacking cone this is the speed made good to windward along the course's axis, so going out to a layline pays like pointing at the mark (Max's wish). On top: 50 m for every step completed, 100 m more at the finish, 5 m less for every tack and gybe, 50 m for a fall, 50 m for OCS, and 2 m for every second. All in metres times `progress_per_m` (0.01), the personality knobs of `RaceEnv.Options`. The old reward (metres closer to the next mark, with 50 and 200 as bonuses and a cost per decision) is gone; its tests were rewritten.

**2. What the head sees, ten numbers more (`race_observation.gd`):** the point the course pulls toward (its distance, its bearing from the bow and from the wind), the direction of the leg after the next step from the wind, the sailing distance still to go, and what the hands were last told (the wind angle wanted and the hold-back speed), so a brain without a memory of its own knows the tack it meant to be on. 68 numbers now (58 before). **Brains pick their inputs by name** (`NeuralNet.select_inputs()`), so the night brains of October still sail in the game and in `tools/gens.sh`, taking their 58 out of the 68.

**3. The hold-back action (`learned_skipper.gd`).** The third output of a wind-angle brain is the hands' hold-back skill (F2's `AiHands.hold_speed_ms`): nothing at 0 or below, 8 m/s just above 0 down to 1.5 m/s at 1. A brain says it has it by naming its third output `hold`; the night brains (`unused`) never hold back. A brain's note also says how often it decides (`decision_ticks`: 30 for the trainer's brains, a decision every half second, as D1 wants the head at 2 Hz).

**4. Drops and respawns (`race_env.gd`, `race_sim.gd`).** `RaceSim.place_board()` puts a board anywhere on the course with the steps before counted as done; `RaceEnv.reset_dropped()` starts a race from a list of drops (a spot, a heading, the next step, and a goal step that ends the episode as a finish); `respawn()` sets a done board down again for a new episode in the same race while the others sail on; `set_scripted()` puts a scripted character on a board instead of the network (the yardstick on the same drops, or company).

**5. Random courses (`random_courses.gd`).** One of the ten templates at 40 to 100 % of the class's size (55 %), or a random windward-leeward (mark 1 300 to 1100 m up, a gate or a leeward mark, one or two rounds, a reach to the finish), a random trapezoid, or a random slalom (a reaching start, mark 1 across the wind, a run, 0 to 3 more marks zigzagging down the wind), for 8 to 24 boards, set to a random wind direction, the start line at least 60 m.

**6. The curriculum (`training_drops.gd`).** The six stages of D6b as presets: S1 the beat (150 to 600 m below a windward mark, 200 m either side, any heading; the rounding or 90 s), S2 the rounding (60 to 150 m from any mark; 60 s), S3 the run (the marks reached on a run and the gates; 60 s), S4 full laps (just past the line, started; 8 min; waves, gusts and shifts), S5 the start (random spots in F2's sailing area, a countdown of 45 to 90 s, the line's bias up to 10 degrees, the boards seeing each other; 120 s after the gun or mark 1), S6 company (8 boards, the countdown, the whole race; the race ends 60 s after the first finisher). Every episode is a new random course in a random wind of 10 to 24 kt. S1 to S3 sail flat water in a steady wind (twice as fast; the hands handle the waves); S4 to S6 the real water and wind.

**7. The bridge (`train_bridge.gd`, `Game/dev/train_worker.gd`, `tools/train/bridge.py`).** RD3 as planned: one GDScript class with the messages (hello, reset, step), one headless worker script that sends and receives them as JSON lines over a TCP socket, one Python class that is a Stable-Baselines3 `VecEnv` over all the workers' boards. Each worker runs one race of a stage with several boards (4 by default), all sailed by the one network, each board an environment of the vector: 14 workers of 4 boards are 56 environments. A board whose episode is over is set down again at once in S1 to S4 (its episode's last observation and stats go back with the step); in S5 and S6 it waits, parked, for the race to end for everyone (it keeps reporting its last observation, with no reward and no end: a few idle steps the trainer sees; bounded by the finish window). No sockets in the unit tests: `TrainBridge` is tested as plain function calls, and the socket end to end by `python tools/train/bridge.py` (two workers, ten random steps).

**8. PPO (`tools/train/train.py`, `tools/train.sh`).** Stable-Baselines3's PPO with an MlpPolicy of two hidden layers of 64 (tanh), a linear action head, the noise starting at 0.37 (`log_std_init` −1), 128 decisions per environment per rollout, batches of 512, 10 epochs, a learning rate of 3e-4, the discount 0.995 for the short stages and 0.998 for the long ones, the rewards normalised (`VecNormalize`, rewards only, so the exported policy is untouched). One line per rollout (the reward, the share of episodes that reached their goal, the speed made good, falls and manoeuvres per episode, the noise) on the screen and in `.sim_output/train/<run>/curve.csv`; every half hour and at the end the model (`model.zip`, to continue the run with the same command) and a brain file `<run>_gen<N>.json` for the game and `tools/gens.sh`, plus `<run>_best.json` (the best mean reward over the last 300 episodes) and `<run>_latest.json`. `--resume=<brain.json>` starts a stage from another run's brain (the value network afresh); `--resume=auto` continues the run's own model. Stops after `--hours`.

**8b. The nights unattended (`--stages`).** `tools/train.sh --stages=S1,S2,S3,S4,S5,S6 --hours_each=4` runs the stages in turn (the runs s1 to s6), each from the one before's best brain, each ended when its "done when" number of D6b's table holds at a check (every hour, the latest brain on 20 fresh drops through the evaluation of item 10, against the all-rounder where the number needs it; every two hours and on fewer drops for S4 to S6, whose episodes take minutes) or after its hours. The numbers: S1 the speed made good 80 % of the all-rounder's; S2 and S3 80 % of the drops rounded within 40 s; S4 90 % of the laps finished within 1.3 times the all-rounder's time on the same drops; S5 90 % of the starts within 10 s of the gun with OCS under 10 % (the episode stats now carry the start's time and OCS); S6 only ends by its hours (beating the all-rounder more often than not is a race, `tools/race.sh`). The checks go to `.sim_output/train/<run>/checks.log`; a stage that was interrupted continues its own model when the command is run again. Added after Max's "I have no time, continue" (21 October 2026, afternoon), so the laptop can work through the curriculum on its own.

**9. The brain export and its proof (`tools/train/export.py`, `parity.py`, `test_neural_net.gd`).** The policy's action side is exactly a `NeuralNet` (tanh hidden layers, a linear last layer; the game clips each output to −1 to 1 as it uses it, as the trainer clips the sampled actions). RD4's parity test: `parity.py` exports a small torch policy with 20 random inputs and torch's outputs to `Game/tests/unit/fixtures/brain_parity.json`; the GDScript network agrees to 1e-5 (measured: better than 1e-6).

**10. The evaluation without Python (`tools/train.sh --evaluate=<brain> --stage=S1 --episodes=20 --reference=allrounder`).** A brain sails a stage's drops, one line per episode and a summary (the mean reward, the share that reached the goal and their mean time, the speed made good, falls and manoeuvres per episode); with `--reference` the scripted character sails the same drops after it and the brain's speed made good is given as a share of the character's: S1's "done when" (80 % of the all-rounder's) is read straight off it. `--track=<file.csv>` writes the first episode's track.

## Measured

- **The all-rounder on S1's drops** (3 episodes, seed 1, `--reference=allrounder`): 4.8 to 5.8 m/s of sailing distance made good (431 m in 90 s on a random trapezoid at 15 kt; mark 1 of a US3 at 56 % rounded in 53.6 s at 20 kt), 1 manoeuvre per episode, no falls. So S1's target for the head is about 4.2 m/s. The night brain `night2b_best` (fed by name) makes −2.4 m/s on the same drops: it only ever saw T1 from one spot.
- **The socket bridge:** two workers of two boards connect in 0.6 s and run ten steps in under 3 s, Godot's start-up included.
- **The trainer's first try** (`--stage=S1 --run=try1 --minutes=8 --workers=10`, 40 boards, with the unit suite running on the same laptop): the loop runs; 170 decisions a second (145 alone on 10 workers of 4 boards; 14 workers should give about 200); but the reward stayed flat at -2.5 for 8 minutes (400 episodes), because a fresh policy's mean output is 0, which in the wind-angle mode is head to wind: every board sat in irons, and the noise (0.37) crossing 0 asked for a tack every few seconds (5 per episode). **Fixed in the trainer:** a fresh policy's wind-angle output starts at 0.3 (a close reach on starboard; `--twa_bias`) with a noise of 0.22 (`--log_std_init=-1.5`). **The second try** (`--run=try2`, 14 minutes, 10 workers): 117,000 decisions and 640 episodes; the reward over the last 300 episodes from -1.3 to -0.18, the speed made good from 0.77 to 1.95 m/s, the tacks from 4.1 to 2.5 per episode, falls to 0.01, the noise from 0.22 to 0.18, every rollout better than the last. The saved brain, run without its noise on 10 fresh drops against the all-rounder: 2.50 m/s, **54 % of the all-rounder's** 4.68 m/s, 0.7 manoeuvres and no falls per episode, no rounding yet (the all-rounder rounds 4 of 10 within the 90 s). So the head learns on the real physics at a rate that makes S1's 80 % a matter of hours, not nights. The curves are in `.sim_output/train/try1/curve.csv` and `try2/curve.csv`; the try brains were not kept.
- **The unit tests:** 505 of 505 (34 more than the night's 471: `test_sailing_distance.gd` 9, `test_training_drops.gd` 8, `test_train_bridge.gd` 5, `test_race_env.gd` rewritten with 14, the parity test, the skipper's hold and by-name inputs, the template course); `tools/check.sh` 250 files, 0 failed. The validation suite was not run (nothing in `Game/sim/` changed).
