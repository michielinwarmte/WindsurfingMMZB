# Review: the hull, the board, the fin's place and the mass model (PH16, branch `ph28/sail-and-hull`)

Independent physics review, 30 October 2026. Scope: `Game/sim/hull_model.gd`, `buoyancy_model.gd`, `mass_model.gd`, `config/board_config.gd`, `config/fin_config.gd`, `Game/config/board_default.tres`, `board_freeride_240.tres`, `fin_default.tres`, and in `windsurfer_sim.gd` only the footstraps (`_press_within_straps`, commit 8d13c5c) and the board-shape parts (567d8d5). Diffed against `origin/main` (4b9297f), HEAD d1e583d. Read-only: nothing in the repo was changed but this file. Numbers below marked "probe" come from running `Game/dev/hull_probe.gd` on this branch; the unit tests named were run.

## Verdict, in short

1. **The new default board's planing lift is not Savitsky's any more; it is the hull model's "no strip may pull" rule acting on the JP SL 72's scoop rocker.** On the old board that rule did nothing at the wet lengths the board rode at (the probe shows 0 N added up to 1.5 m of wet length). On the new board it adds lift at every wet length the board rides at, and at 2 degrees of heel the lift leaps from 0.74 to 2.1 to 3.2 times Savitsky's between 1.0, 1.28 and 1.5 m wet. The report's own section 7 says this gain "is not to be trusted"; section 9 then made the board the default anyway. The 4 to 6 % speed gain of the slalom shape, and the "1.2 to 1.3 m wet length" that PH16 is now measuring against, both come from this rule. Until Michiel decides what a strip on a curved bottom may do (the spec's assumption A-05), the SL 72's speeds and wet lengths are not evidence. This is the one finding that matters.
2. The cut-outs are off by default, as claimed, and the footstraps are built correctly: the right sign, the right distances, on for the AI boards too, and tested. The fin's place converts correctly (0.18 m from the tail is z 0.96 on a 2.28 m board); its sweep is an estimate and labelled as one.
3. The stiff fin's validation pin was re-pinned five times on this branch (three times in commits within this scope). It is a regression pin by design, so each move was "the target moved to match the output". Nothing independent checks the fin's new place.
4. The mass model is sound (no NaN paths; the 80 kg composite floats at Archimedes' depth in the sim test), but no unit test pins the shipped board's grid or its lift against Savitsky: the hand-worked tests were pointed at the old board, which is fair, and nothing took their place for the new one.
5. The spec's tables still describe the old gear in several places; the tuning log has the changes, the sections do not.

The unit tests in scope all pass: `buoyancy` (52 asserts), `hull` (209), `footstraps` (10), `mass_model` (23), `board_cutouts` (10), `windsurfer_sim` (17 tests, 89 asserts). I did not run the validation suite (another reviewer does).

## Findings, by severity

### 1. Wrong physics / artefact: the default board rides on the strips' clip (`hull_model.gd:515-537`)

**What the rule is.** The planing lift is computed strip by strip (slender-body theory) and anchored to Savitsky's measured lift at the reference trim (`_apply_planing_lift`, `hull_model.gd:365-546`). Step 6 of it drops every strip whose force comes out negative: `quasi_steady_n += maxf(three_d_factor * (...), 0.0)` at line 525 and `if magnitude <= 0.0: continue` at line 536. The spec lists this as assumption A-05, "a planing bottom ventilates rather than sucking", with the note "nothing needed" (PHYSICS_SPEC.md:6807). The rule is unchanged on this branch (the whole `hull_model.gd` diff is two lines, `_width_at` taking `bottom_width_at`).

**Why it matters now.** Where the wet front sits on the rocker, the spray root meets the water at the pitch plus the rocker's local slope, so the root's push is large; the strips behind it, where the bottom flattens out and lifts away, would pull in a consistent strip theory and cancel most of that push, so that the total follows the angle at the aft end (the report's section 7 explains this well). The clip drops the pull and keeps the push. The old board was flat to 1.5 m from the tail and rode at 1.4 to 1.7 m wet, so the clip touched only its last few centimetres. The new board's rocker starts 0.68 m from the tail (`board_default.tres`: `nose_rocker_m` 0.128 over `nose_rocker_length_m` 1.6, on a 2.28 m board), with a slope of 2.6 degrees at 1.14 m and 3.4 degrees at 1.27 m, so every planing wet length (1.2 to 1.3 m per report section 14) ends on it.

**Probe, same speed (14.97 m/s, the 18 kt beam reach of section 14), trim 1.0 degree.** "clip" is what dropping the pulling strips adds back (2D, before the 3D factor); "lift / Sav" is the model's planing lift over Savitsky's at the same wet length and beam.

Three things follow:
- The outline is not the cause (the report's section 6 said so; the probe confirms it: the SL 72 outline with the old rocker behaves like the old board). The rocker through the clip is the whole difference.
- At the heel the board sails at (about 2 degrees), the old board was Savitsky's or below at every wet length it rode at (the clip added 23 N at 1.7 m). The new board is 2.1 times Savitsky at 1.28 m and 3.2 times at 1.5 m, and 0.74 times at 1.0 m: the lift rises 3.6-fold over 0.28 m of wet length. That is a wall. It is what holds the wet front at 1.2 to 1.3 m, not the moment balance alone; section 14 half says this ("the wet front settles where the rocker lifts the bottom out") and then files the wet length under "the moment balance, Michiel's". A heave stiffness that steep may also be what pumps the pitch (section 14's budget: the hull's water puts +4.5 W into the pitch). That last sentence is my reading, not a measurement.
- The report's "about 70 % more lift where thin-wing theory gives 10 to 20 %" (section 7) is at 1.22 m level; at the ridden heel it is 110 % at 1.28 m. Both are the report's and my readings of the same probe; no measurement of a rockered planing bottom exists in the project's sources (the report says so, and the spec's note at PHYSICS_SPEC.md:2481 already recorded 1.44 times Savitsky on the old board at 1.76 m from the same cause, "not changed, reported").

**What to do.** This is a hull-model decision, Michiel's (the report's section 8, item 7, asks exactly this): may a strip pull (consistent strip theory, thin-wing theory), may it not (ventilation, A-05), or a bound in between (suction no larger than the water's head above the bottom plus a sourced ventilation limit). Until it is decided: (a) the reach-check speeds, the wet lengths and the "second lock" of section 14 on the SL 72 should be labelled as resting on A-05, not as measurements of the board; (b) a hull test on the shipped board at its own state against Savitsky would fail today, which is the honest state of things (see finding 7); (c) the lead should know that section 9 shipped the shape one section after section 7 called its gain untrustworthy. I propose no number: the choice is physics, not tuning.

### 2. Decision bypassed: the fin at 0.18 m was "measured, not built: for Michiel" in section 13, then built in section 14 (`fin_default.tres`, commit 7b53d4d)

Section 13 reports that moving the fin's force to Max's fin box alone makes the 24 kt beam reach lose its course and catapult, and leaves it "for Michiel: with the fin where the box puts it, what holds the course and the trim?" Section 14 (the same session, the same day) then makes the fin's place the default together with the mast foot moved 5.5 cm aft to 1.265 m (`sail_default.tres`, `mast_foot_local` z -0.125), the hands at 0.70 m and 80 kg, and the whole sails without falls. The compensating change is Max's own setting (he said "around 1.2", read as the back of his track, which runs 1.245 to 1.405 m), so it is sourced; but the balance question posed for Michiel was answered by shipping a setup that happens to work, and the 24 kt beam reach bounces at 0.83 degrees pitch SD with the mast foot at 1.245 m (section 13's table) against 0.00 at 1.265 m (section 14's). That sensitivity (2 cm of mast foot) is itself a sign that the balance is on an edge. For the lead and Michiel to note; nothing to change in the code for it.

The conversion is right: 0.5 x 2.28 - 0.18 = 0.96 (`fin_config.gd:12-16`, `fin_default.tres` `root_local` z 0.96; the body's z is aft-positive, the bow is -Z, rule 5). The sweep estimate ("a swept 39 cm slalom fin's quarter chord at 40 % of the span is about 0.15 to 0.22 m from the tail") is labelled an estimate, and the chord and sweep are not measured; the model's own root chord is 0.140 m (`root_chord_m()`), which puts the root's quarter chord at about 0.22 m for a base starting 2 cm ahead of the front screw; 0.18 m then assumes some 0.04 m of aft sweep at 40 % span, which is plausible for a slalom fin but is a guess until Max names the fin. Cosmetic: the root's y stays -0.06 while the hull's bottom is now at -0.065 (thickness 0.13), so the root sits 5 mm inside the hull; the old 0.12 m board had it exactly on the bottom.

### 3. Untested / test target moved: the stiff fin's pin (`Game/tests/validation/test_fin_bend.gd:28-43`)

`STIFF_SLIP_DEG`, `STIFF_LIFT_N` and `STIFF_UP_N` were moved on this branch in 5b74e27, 435089a, 818396d, 0666ad6 and 7b53d4d (now 1.72 degrees, 652 N, 22 N; on main 1.64, 475, 16). The test's own comments say it is a pin of the stiff fin's numbers on the 18 kt beam reach, re-pinned whenever the gear changes, and the bending fin is then compared against it; so moving it is its documented practice, and the comment trail is complete and honest. But a pin moved five times in four days guards nothing across those changes: no test checks the fin's new place (0.18 m), its lever on the yaw balance, or the 652 N it now carries (the polar's 650 N plausibility band went red for the same reason, section 14). Either the pin should be labelled a regression record rather than a validation, or the fin needs a check against something outside the model (the report's section 10 says Fagg 1997 had nothing on the vertical force; the side force's band in `test_polar_sweep.gd` has no source either).

### 4. Footstraps (8d13c5c): correct and on everywhere; one robustness note

**What it does** (`windsurfer_sim.gd:1119-1127`, `_copy_configs` at 467-468, `mass_model.gd:135-137`). The straps are converted from "from the tail" to body z as `0.5 * length - from_tail`: the back strap at 0.235 m is z 0.905, the front at 0.825 m is z 0.315 (aft is +z, correct under rule 5). After each tick's trimming, the trim press (how far aft of the stance the feet's pressure is) is clamped so that stance + press goes no further aft than the back strap, and, once the stance itself is aft of the front strap, no further forward than the front strap. A board with 0 in either field gets INF / -INF, no limit (the arithmetic with INF is safe in `clampf`). The body moves with the press (`_set_posture`, line 943), so the weight stops there too; the hang-back (the body leaning back from the feet) is not clamped, as documented. Physically this is the right idea: a rider in the straps can put all the weight on one foot and no more.

**Signs and distances, checked**: the stances were re-based so the feet keep their distances from the tail on the shorter board (`sailor_default.tres` 0.09 / 0.54 / -0.26 = 1.05 / 0.60 / 1.40 m from the tail, the same as 0.15 / 0.60 / -0.20 on 2.40 m). The planing stance (0.60 m from the tail) lies between the straps (0.235 and 0.825), so at stance 1 the press may go 0.365 m aft and 0.225 m forward. The report's reach check says the steady press sits at about 0.27 m: inside the strap.

**AI boards (rule 7)**: `race_env.gd:468` builds every board with `WindsurferSim.create_default()`, which loads `board_default.tres` (`windsurfer_sim.gd:304-321`); with `own_copies` the resources are duplicated with the same values. So the straps are on for the fleet.

**Test**: `test_footstraps.gd` pins the default's strap places, the aft stop, the forward stop, free movement between them, the rest stance not held back, and no straps. All 10 asserts pass.

**Robustness note (low; not observed).** The forward limit switches from "none" to "the front strap" the moment the stance passes the front strap. If the press were already forward then, it would be snapped to the strap in one substep. The hump speed is only 2.6 m/s (`_board_residuary_peak_froude` 0.55 on 2.28 m) while the stance moves between 3.5 and 7 m/s, so the two could overlap. I ran the default from rest at 12 and 18 kt on a beam and a broad reach (a scratch script, 40 s each) and logged the largest one-tick change of the press: never more than the rate limit (5 mm per 1/60 s). The reason is the hump rule at `windsurfer_sim.gd:1037-1040`: above hump speed with the bow low, the stance target is 1, so the sailor is fully in the straps by 3.6 m/s, before any press builds. So no bug today; if the stance rule ever changes, this clamp would want a rate limit after it.

### 5. The board's numbers (`board_default.tres`, 567d8d5, 818396d): consistent; estimates labelled

- **Volume.** The buoyancy grid does not integrate the shape: it apportions the config's `volume_m3` (0.117) over the rows by their section width x thickness (`buoyancy_model.gd:120-144`), so 117 l is exact by construction. As a check of the shape itself: the outline times the thickness profile integrates to 144 l (midpoint rule, 2000 steps); 117 l is 0.81 of that, which is what rounded rails and a domed deck take off a rectangular section. Plausible; not a source.
- **Mass** 7.8 kg: "6.6 published plus straps and fin, an estimate" (report section 9, the spec's tuning log). Labelled. The fin's mass is lumped into the hull's box inertia (`mass_model.gd:67`); fine at this level.
- **Outline**: widest 1.14 m from the tail, 0.49 m one foot from the tail, 0.56 m one foot from the nose, traced from JP's photo (the trace's scale checked against 72/228 within 2 %). The model's tail is then 0.322 m wide at the transom and 0.452 m at the back straps (Max's Patrik measures 0.47 m there; a different board). 
- **Rocker**: the parabola 0.128 m over 1.6 m, fitted from 1.0 to 1.9 m, gives 11, 17, 42, 63, 87, 115 mm at 1.14, 1.27, 1.6, 1.8, 2.0, 2.2 m against the trace's 7, (16 at 1.3), 36, 60, 93, 156 mm: good where the board meets the water, 80 mm low at the nose tip (128 against 208). The nose only matters for a nose dive (`_apply_bow_drag` uses the nose row's depth), where the model's nose is lower than the real one: minor.
- **Thickness** 0.13 / 0.045 / 0.04 "traced where the straps do not hide it": labelled as traced.
- **Origin and units agree** among the config, the buoyancy, the hull and the mass model: the origin is the centre of the hull box; the bottom's flat is at y = -0.065 (`buoyancy_model.gd:127`, `hull_model.gd:868`); the mass model's box is centred on the origin; the mast foot's y 0.081 is the deck at 1.265 m from the tail (-0.065 + the rocker's 0.017 + the thickness there 0.129 = 0.081, checked). SI units throughout.
- **Two pre-existing simplifications that the thinner tail makes larger** (minor, for the spec rather than the code): the feet stand at y = 0.5 x `thickness_m` = 0.065 everywhere (`mass_model.gd:152`), but the deck at the back straps is at 0.011 (the tail is 0.076 thick there), so the feet are 5 cm high at the straps; and every buoyancy column fills over the full 0.13 m (`buoyancy_model.gd:195`, spec 6.3), also the 4.5 cm thick tail, so the stern is credited with less buoyancy than its thin tail would give at a given depth. Both are second order when planing.

### 6. Cut-outs: off by default, as claimed; the 3D model draws them anyway

`board_default.tres` does not set `cutout_from_tail_m` or `cutout_depth_m`, so both are 0 (`board_config.gd:90-91`), and `bottom_width_at` returns `width_at` unchanged (line 205-209; `test_board_cutouts.gd` checks it). The hull (`_width_at`, `hull_model.gd:810`), the buoyancy grid and `planform_area_m2` all take `bottom_width_at`, so a board with cut-outs would get them in the lift's beam, the friction's area and the buoyancy consistently. Cosmetic inconsistency: the Blender model draws cut-outs of 0.40 m by 2 cm on the JP (progress file, step 19) while the physics has none; the visuals never feed the physics (rule 2), but a player who sees cut-outs will think they are simulated.

### 7. Untested: nothing pins the shipped board's grid or lift

`test_buoyancy.gd` and `test_hull.gd` were pointed at `board_freeride_240.tres` (the old default, byte for byte). That is fair: they pin hand-worked numbers of that shape (planform 1.346 m2, the 21 volumes summing to 120 l, Savitsky at a known state). But nothing took their place for the shipped board: no test checks its planform (1.371 m2), its rows on the rocker, its resting depth (the sim test does pin Archimedes for the whole 97.3 kg: `test_windsurfer_sim.gd:48`), or, the one that matters, its planing lift against Savitsky at the state it rides at (`test_hull.gd:109` runs on the old board). Such a test would fail today (finding 1), which is the point of writing it: it would state what A-05 does to this board and go green when Michiel's decision is built. `test_mass_model.gd` now overrides the sailor back to 75 kg and the old stances (lines 6-15), so the shipped 80 kg composite has no unit pin either; the hand calculations are still valid for what they test.

### 8. Mass model and NaN safety (`mass_model.gd`)

The diff only factors the stance's z into `stance_z_m()` (lines 135-137), used by `update()` and `feet_position_body()`; the arithmetic is the same as before. With the 80 kg default the composite is 97.3 kg (7.8 + 9.5 + 80); at rest in the sim test it displaces 94.9 l of the 117, 81 % submerged, which is what a slalom board with an 80 kg rider does standing still. No division by speed or by a depth anywhere in the mass model; `total_mass_kg` is always positive; the inertia is built part by part with the parallel axis theorem (tested). The INF strap limits never enter the mass model. I found no path to NaN at zero speed or in a fall in these files (the NaN that was found on the branch was in the rig's water drag, outside my scope, and is fixed in 782f3e5).

### 10. Cosmetic
