What works

  results after 4 and 7 eliminations) and the standings, and is saved after every heat.
- The TV director films it with the stream's measured numbers; the commentator has about 740 of
  the real commentator's lines (from the 27 h of streams and the eleven heat replays, judged by
  hand), and for the event's own moments 39 of his sentences and 47 lines written for the game

  countdown the boards cross the line about 6 s after the gun (nine in ten within 15 s).

What is not right yet
- The AI's gybes: a board loses most of its speed at every mark (about 5 s a mark against the
  real sailors), and at mark 1, where the whole fleet arrives together, one to three of the eight
  are pushed inside the mark and cannot get back. This is AI training for Sotavento (you said: "yes but
  later"), not built this night. What the lead did set: every event sailor passes a mark at least
  14 m out (measured: no more missed marks further round the course; --event_mark_margin=0 in a
  launcher gives each sailor his own margin again).
- Because of that, a rule of the lead's (not the tour's): a sailor gives up his heat ("retired")
  when the heat's fourth board rounded the mark he is still sailing for more than 45 s ago.
  Before, such a board circled its mark for minutes. Off with --event_give_up=0 in a launcher.
- Now and then (7 times in about 80 test heats) a board's physics breaks: its turning speed goes to
  infinity in one step. Twice that hung a heat. The race now puts such a board back at its start
  spot and retires it (one warning in the log), so the broadcast goes on. Why the physics does
  it is not known: a question for Michiel.
- The ranking decides little yet. A heat's best seeds start from the spots beside the start boat
  (set at 04:15: before, the best seed had the row's far spot and crossed the line 12 s after the
  gun, the last seed 2 s after it, and all eight finalists of a test elimination came from the
  worse half of the ranking). Now the first quarter of the ranking goes through from 58 % of its
  heats and the last quarter from 38 % (13 test heats), but the favourites, first at mark 1, are
  also the ones who most often lose it there (31 % of them). The provisional sailors keep their
  characters' daring, and a daring tail-ender still wins heats. The roster is yours to make.
